## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- explain which public bodies collect taxes and social security contributions in Angola;
- classify the main taxes a business meets and the payments each one applies to;
- apply the withholding principle: who bears a tax, who deducts it and who delivers it;
- check that NIFs and identification data on payroll and invoices are complete.

## Key terms

::: terms
AGT: Administração Geral Tributária, the state body that administers and collects taxes in Angola
INSS: Instituto Nacional de Segurança Social, the body that registers employers and workers and collects social security contributions
NIF: Número de Identificação Fiscal, the tax identification number of a person or company
Withholding: deducting tax from a payment at source and delivering it to the State on behalf of the person who earns the income
Taxpayer: the person or company that bears a tax under the law
Diário da República: the official gazette in which laws and decrees are published
:::

## 1. Two collectors: AGT and INSS

Every monthly payroll in Angola creates obligations towards two public bodies. The AGT administers taxes: it registers taxpayers, receives returns, collects payments and carries out audits. The INSS registers employers and workers and collects the contributions that fund pensions and other benefits. Each has its own registrations, returns and deadlines.

The rules come from laws and decrees published in the Diário da República. Tax codes, such as the Código do IRT and the Código do IVA, set the permanent rules. The annual State Budget law (Lei do Orçamento Geral do Estado, or OGE) often changes rates and limits for that year. For 2026 the OGE is Lei n.º 14/25, of 30 December 2025, which introduced a new IRT table from 1 January 2026.

On 25 June 2026 the National Assembly approved a new personal income tax code, the IRPS (Imposto sobre o Rendimento das Pessoas Singulares), which is expected to replace the current IRT model from 1 January 2027. This course teaches the 2026 rules, but its methods will still apply under the new code.

::: tip | Check the current law
Rates, tables and limits in this course were checked against 2025–2026 sources. They change often, so confirm every figure on the AGT and INSS websites or in the Diário da República before you use it.
:::

## 2. The main taxes a business meets

Classify each tax by what it hits: income from work, profit, consumption, property or transactions.

- IRT (Imposto sobre o Rendimento do Trabalho): tax on income from work. Group A covers employees. Group B covers self-employed professionals and company managers and officers. Group C covers income of individuals from industrial and commercial activities.
- Imposto Industrial: tax on the profits of companies.
- IVA (Imposto sobre o Valor Acrescentado): tax on consumption, charged on sales of goods and services. The standard rate is 14%.
- Other taxes: Imposto de Selo (stamp duty), Imposto Predial (property) and IAC (investment income such as interest and dividends).

Payroll staff work mostly with Group A. When a payer with organised or simplified accounts pays a self-employed professional (Group B), IRT is generally withheld at 6.5% of the service value.

::: quiz
? A company pays a self-employed electrician who has no employment contract. Which IRT group applies to his fee?
- Group A, because he works for the company || Group A is only for employees working under an employment relationship.
* Group B, because he is a self-employed professional || Correct. Fees of self-employed professionals fall under Group B, usually with withholding by the company.
- Group C, because electricity is an industrial activity || Group C covers individuals running industrial or commercial businesses, not professional fees.
:::

## 3. The withholding principle and the NIF

For any payment, ask: who bears the tax, who deducts it, and who delivers it by when? For IRT Group A, the employee bears the tax, the employer deducts it when paying the salary, and the employer delivers it to the AGT by the end of the month following payment (Código do IRT, article 11).

::: slides
SLIDE Step 1: Identify the payee | Employee, self-employed professional or company?; Check the contract and the NIF | The payee decides the rule
SLIDE Step 2: Identify the income type | Salary, fee, rent, interest or a sale?; Find the tax and the group | One payment, one classification
SLIDE Step 3: Check the current rule | Table or rate in force for this month; Exemptions and limits | Never rely on memory
SLIDE Step 4: Deduct, record and deliver | Deduct on payment; Post to a tax payable account; Pay the State by the deadline | Withheld tax is a debt to the State
:::

Withheld tax is held in trust for the State. Under the 2026 budget law, failing to deliver withheld IRT, or delivering less than was withheld, carries a fine equal to the amount not delivered (reduced from twice that amount), and criminal proceedings may still apply.

The NIF must appear in employee master data, on invoices and in returns. A purchase invoice without the buyer's correct NIF can cost the company its right to deduct input IVA (lesson 5).

::: diagram steps | From payslip to the State
Calculate: the employer calculates gross pay
Deduct: employee INSS and IRT are withheld
Pay: net pay goes to the employee
Deliver to INSS: contributions by the 10th of the following month
Deliver to AGT: IRT by the end of the following month
:::

::: quiz
? Who delivers the IRT withheld from an employee's salary to the AGT?
- The employee, through an annual return || The employee bears the tax, but delivery is the employer's duty.
- The bank that transfers the salary || Banks process payments but are not the withholding agent for salaries.
* The employer, by the end of the following month || Correct. The employer withholds and delivers the tax.
:::

## Worked example: a new distributor in Viana

A small distributor of cleaning products in Viana, Luanda, starts trading with eight employees. In month one it pays salaries, a self-employed IT technician (Kz 200,000) and office rent. The bookkeeper builds a tax map. Salaries: IRT Group A by the table, plus INSS (3% employee, 8% employer). IT technician: Group B, so she withholds 6.5%, Kz 13,000, pays him Kz 187,000 and records Kz 13,000 as IRT payable. Rent: she confirms the applicable tax with the accountant before paying. Two employees have no NIF on file, so she collects proof before payroll closes.

::: case | Cash is tight at the Viana distributor
In the second month a supplier demands Kz 900,000. The owner tells the bookkeeper, Teresa, to use the Kz 420,000 of withheld IRT and INSS to pay the supplier and to "settle the State next month".
? What should Teresa do?
- Follow the instruction, because the owner decides on payments || The money withheld belongs to the State and the INSS, so using it exposes the company to fines and interest.
* Explain that withheld amounts are debts to the State and propose another way to pay the supplier || Correct. She protects the company and offers a solution such as a payment plan with the supplier.
- Pay the supplier and record nothing until next month || Hiding the liability makes the problem worse and breaks basic accounting controls.
- Deliver only the IRT and keep the INSS money || Both amounts are withheld from workers and carry penalties if delivered late.
:::

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- identify which payments to an employee are taxable under IRT Group A;
- exclude non-taxable allowances correctly, taxing only the excess over a limit;
- calculate monthly taxable income after the employee's INSS contribution;
- apply the IRT table method to calculate the tax to withhold.

## Key terms

::: terms
Gross remuneration: everything paid to the employee for work in the month, in cash or in kind
Non-taxable income: amounts the IRT Code excludes from tax, sometimes only up to a limit
Taxable income (matéria colectável): the amount to which the IRT table is applied
Bracket (escalão): a band of income with its own fixed amount and rate
Fixed amount (parcela fixa): the tax set by the table for income up to the lower limit of the bracket
Marginal rate: the rate applied to the part of income above the lower limit of the bracket
:::

## 1. What is taxable under Group A

Group A covers employees. The IRT Code taxes salaries, wages, overtime, bonuses and other additional remuneration, and benefits in kind (for example a company house or car used privately). The starting point is everything the employee received for work in the month.

Some amounts are not taxable, at least up to a limit. According to the IRT Code and the PwC Angola tax summary (reviewed June 2026), the main items are:

- Meal and transport allowances, up to Kz 30,000 per month. Sources differ on whether the limit applies to each allowance or to both together, so confirm with your accountant or the AGT. The part above the limit is taxable.
- Family allowance, up to 5% of base salary.
- Business travel expenses, when properly documented.
- The employee's own social security contributions.

The key method is "limit, then excess". If an employee receives a meal allowance of Kz 45,000 and no transport allowance, Kz 30,000 is not taxable and Kz 15,000 is added to taxable income.

::: quiz
? An employee receives a transport allowance of Kz 42,000 and no meal allowance. How much of it is taxable?
- Kz 42,000, because allowances are always taxable || Allowances are non-taxable up to the legal limit.
- Nothing, because transport allowances are exempt || The exclusion applies only up to Kz 30,000 a month.
* Kz 12,000, the part above the Kz 30,000 limit || Correct. Only the excess over the limit is taxed.
:::

## 2. From gross to taxable income

The IRT Code (article 7) deducts the employee's mandatory social security contribution from total remuneration before the table is applied. The formula is:

Taxable income = gross remuneration - non-taxable amounts - employee INSS contribution

The employee INSS contribution is 3% of the contribution base (lesson 3). Calculate it first, because IRT depends on it.

## 3. The table method

Angola's IRT is progressive: higher income falls into higher brackets. Each bracket has a lower limit, a fixed amount and a marginal rate. The formula for every bracket is:

IRT = fixed amount + marginal rate x (taxable income - lower limit of the bracket)

The 2026 table (Annex I of Lei n.º 14/25, as reproduced by PwC and other professional sources) is, per month:

- Up to Kz 150,000: exempt.
- Kz 150,000 to 200,000: Kz 12,500 + 16% of the excess over Kz 150,000.
- Kz 200,000 to 300,000: Kz 31,250 + 18% of the excess over Kz 200,000.
- Kz 300,000 to 500,000: Kz 49,250 + 19% of the excess over Kz 300,000.
- Kz 500,000 to 1,000,000: Kz 87,250 + 20% of the excess over Kz 500,000.
- Kz 1,000,000 to 1,500,000: Kz 187,250 + 21% of the excess over Kz 1,000,000.
- Higher brackets continue with rates of 22%, 23%, 24% and 24.5%, up to 25% above Kz 10,000,000.

Note that the published fixed amounts in the lower brackets do not follow a smooth pattern: tax jumps to Kz 12,500 as soon as taxable income passes Kz 150,000. Apply the table exactly as published and confirm unusual results with the AGT.

::: slides
SLIDE Step 1: Add up gross pay | Salary, overtime, bonuses, benefits in kind | Everything paid for work
SLIDE Step 2: Remove non-taxable amounts | Allowances up to their limits, documented travel | Tax only the excess
SLIDE Step 3: Deduct employee INSS | 3% of the contribution base | INSS comes before IRT
SLIDE Step 4: Apply the table | Find the bracket, fixed amount + rate x excess | Check the table date
:::

Example: gross pay of Kz 700,000, all taxable, gives INSS of Kz 21,000 and taxable income of Kz 679,000. This falls in the Kz 500,000 to 1,000,000 bracket, so IRT = 87,250 + 20% x 179,000 = 87,250 + 35,800 = Kz 123,050. A common error is to charge 20% on the whole Kz 679,000 (Kz 135,800).

:::

::: tip | Check the current law

:::

## Worked example: a warehouse supervisor at a Lobito logistics company

A logistics company near Lobito port pays its warehouse supervisor a base salary of Kz 520,000, overtime of Kz 60,000 and a meal allowance of Kz 35,000 (no transport allowance). Gross pay is Kz 615,000. The company includes allowances in the INSS base, so employee INSS is 3% x 615,000 = Kz 18,450. The non-taxable part of the meal allowance is Kz 30,000. Taxable income = 615,000 - 30,000 - 18,450 = Kz 566,550, in the Kz 500,000 to 1,000,000 bracket. IRT = 87,250 + 20% x 66,550 = 87,250 + 13,310 = Kz 100,560. Net pay before other deductions = 615,000 - 18,450 - 100,560 = Kz 495,990.

::: case | A payslip query in Lobito
The supervisor complains that his IRT rose after his meal allowance went from Kz 25,000 to Kz 35,000. The payroll clerk, Rui, must answer him.
? What is the best explanation?
- The whole allowance became taxable once it passed Kz 30,000 || Only the excess over the limit is taxable, not the whole allowance.
* The Kz 5,000 above the Kz 30,000 limit is now taxable, and the extra pay also raises INSS || Correct. He shows the working step by step.
- The company moved him to a higher table by mistake || The table is the same for all employees. The base changed.
:::

## Key terms

::: terms
Social security: the public system that protects workers through pensions and other benefits, funded by contributions
Contribution base: the remuneration on which contribution rates are calculated
Employee contribution: the share deducted from the worker's pay
Employer contribution: the share the company pays on top of gross pay

## 1. Registration and rates

Every employer must register with the INSS and register each worker it hires, so that contributions are credited to the right person. A worker who is not registered may lose benefits even if money was deducted from his pay. Make INSS registration part of the onboarding checklist, together with the contract and the NIF.

The rules are set by Presidential Decree n.º 227/18, of 27 September. The rates in force are:

- Employee: 3% of the contribution base, deducted from pay.
- Employer: 8% of the contribution base, paid by the company.
- Total: 11%.

A law-firm summary of the decree notes that retired workers who continue to work contribute 8%, with the employer also paying 8%.

In 2025 the press reported proposals to raise the rates to 5% for employees and 10% for employers. In mid-2025 the INSS confirmed that this increase was not yet in force. Apply the published decree until a new one appears in the Diário da República.

::: tip | Check the current law
The 3% and 8% rates come from Decree n.º 227/18 and were still reported as in force in 2025–2026. Check the INSS website for any new decree before each new year.
:::

## 2. The contribution base

The base is the worker's gross remuneration, including cash supplements and benefits in kind converted to their cash value. The decree excludes some items. A law-firm summary lists family allowance, holiday payments and complementary social protection. Because exclusions matter, keep a copy of the decree's list next to your payroll settings and review each new pay element against it.

Method: for each worker, add all remuneration items, remove excluded items, and multiply the result by 3% (employee) and 8% (employer).

::: quiz
? A worker's contribution base is Kz 250,000. What are the employee and employer contributions?
- Kz 2,500 and Kz 8,000 || These use 1% and about 3%, not the rates in force.
* Kz 7,500 and Kz 20,000 || Correct: 3% x 250,000 and 8% x 250,000.
- Kz 20,000 and Kz 7,500 || The rates are reversed: the employee pays 3%, the employer 8%.
:::

Contributions follow the same cycle every month.

The decree sets payment by the 10th day of the month after the month the contributions relate to, and a 2026 law-firm labour bulletin confirms that the remuneration sheet and payment are both due by the 10th. The same bulletin notes that companies with more than 20 employees must file contributions electronically. Family allowance paid under Presidential Decree n.º 95/26 must also be shown on the monthly payslip.

::: diagram cycle | INSS contribution cycle

Late payment is expensive. According to the summary of Decree n.º 227/18, default interest is 1% per month on the debt, and administrative fines range from one to eight times the average monthly remuneration.

::: quiz

* 10 April || Correct. The deadline is the 10th of the following month.
- 30 April || The end of the following month is the IRT deadline, not the INSS deadline.

## Worked example: a bakery in Huambo

A bakery in Huambo has 12 workers. The February contribution base, after removing excluded items, is Kz 4,800,000. Employee contributions are 3% x 4,800,000 = Kz 144,000, deducted from pay. Employer contributions are 8% x 4,800,000 = Kz 384,000, a cost to the bakery. The total to pay with the remuneration sheet is Kz 528,000, due by 10 March. The owner considers paying two months late. At 1% default interest per month, interest would be about Kz 5,280 per month, around Kz 10,560 for two months, before any fine. The bookkeeper recommends paying on time and planning cash for the employer share in the monthly budget.

::: case | A new baker without INSS registration
The bakery hires a new baker on 15 February. The manager, Júlia, finds on 5 March that he was never registered with the INSS, but 3% was deducted from his February pay.
? What should Júlia do first?
- Refund the 3% and leave him off the sheet until April || He worked in February, so he must be declared and the employer share is still due.
* Register him now and include him on the February remuneration sheet before the 10th || Correct. This credits his contributions and meets the deadline.
- Keep the deduction and register him at the end of the year || The deduction is already a debt to the INSS and he risks losing benefits.
- Ask the baker to register himself and pay his own share || Registration and payment of both shares are the employer's duty.
:::

## 1. The eight-step gross-to-net method

A reliable payroll follows the same sequence every month. Each step uses the result of the step before, so the order matters.

- Step 1. Collect inputs before the cut-off date: attendance, approved overtime, absences, new hires, leavers, salary changes and advances.
- Step 2. Calculate gross pay item by item: base salary, overtime, allowances, bonuses and benefits in kind.
- Step 3. Calculate the employee INSS contribution: 3% of the contribution base.
- Step 4. Calculate taxable income and IRT with the table method (lesson 2).
- Step 5. Apply other deductions the employee has authorised in writing or that the law requires, such as repayment of a salary advance.
- Step 6. Calculate net pay: gross pay minus all deductions.
- Step 7. Calculate employer cost: gross pay plus 8% employer INSS.
- Step 8. Issue payslips and reconcile totals before payment.

Overtime and absence rules come from the Lei Geral do Trabalho and any collective agreement. Keep the rates your company applies in a written payroll policy, approved by HR, so every clerk calculates them the same way.

## 3. Reconcile before you pay

The gross-to-net reconciliation catches most payroll errors before money leaves the bank. Check four equations:

- Total gross pay - total deductions = total net pay.
- Total net pay = total of the bank transfer file.
- Total IRT withheld = IRT payable to the AGT this month.
- Total employee INSS + employer INSS = amount on the remuneration sheet.

Then compare this month with last month. Every change above a set threshold (for example 5% of total net pay, or any individual change above Kz 100,000) needs an explanation: a new hire, a leaver, a salary increase or overtime.

::: quiz
? Total net pay on the payroll report is Kz 18,640,000 but the bank file totals Kz 18,890,000. What should the clerk do?
- Pay the bank file, because the bank total is usually correct || A difference means someone may be overpaid or missing.
- Adjust the payroll report to match the bank file || Forcing totals to agree hides the error.
* Stop and find the Kz 250,000 difference before payment || Correct. Payroll is not paid until the totals agree.
:::

## Key terms

::: terms
IVA: Imposto sobre o Valor Acrescentado, Angola's value added tax on goods and services
Output IVA: IVA the company charges on its sales
Input IVA: IVA the company pays on its purchases
IVA payable: output IVA minus deductible input IVA for the period
Exempt supply: a sale on which no IVA is charged and for which input IVA cannot be deducted
Periodic return: the monthly declaration of output and input IVA sent to the AGT
:::

## 1. How IVA works: the credit method

IVA is a tax on consumption. Each business in the chain charges IVA on its sales (output IVA), deducts the IVA it paid on its purchases (input IVA) and pays the difference to the AGT. This is the credit method: IVA payable = output IVA - input IVA. If input is higher than output, the company has a credit.

The result is that each business pays tax only on the value it adds, and the final consumer bears the full tax. For the business, IVA collected is not revenue: it is money owed to the State, and input IVA is not a cost when it can be deducted.

::: diagram steps | IVA along a supply chain (standard rate 14%)
Importer sells for Kz 100,000 + 14,000 IVA: pays Kz 14,000
Distributor sells for Kz 150,000 + 21,000 IVA: pays 21,000 - 14,000 = Kz 7,000
Retailer sells for Kz 200,000 + 28,000 IVA: pays 28,000 - 21,000 = Kz 7,000
Consumer pays Kz 228,000: bears all Kz 28,000, which is 14% of Kz 200,000
:::

The importer in the chain also paid IVA at customs on import, which it deducts in the same way. The principle is the same at every stage.

::: quiz
? A distributor charges Kz 2,100,000 output IVA and has Kz 1,400,000 of deductible input IVA in the month. What is the position?
- A credit of Kz 700,000 || A credit arises only when input is higher than output.
* Kz 700,000 payable to the AGT || Correct: 2,100,000 - 1,400,000.
- Kz 2,100,000 payable, because input IVA is a cost || Deductible input IVA reduces the amount payable.
:::

## 2. Rates and regimes

According to the PwC Angola tax summary (reviewed June 2026), the main rates are:

- Standard rate: 14%.
- 5% on food and agricultural inputs listed in Tables I and II of the IVA Code.
- 7% on certain hotel and restaurant services, subject to conditions.
- Special rates of 1% and 2% for some operations in Cabinda.

The 2026 budget law also allows a 5% rate on the import of industrial equipment, on request and with AGT approval, and exempts transactions made through payment platforms and instant mobile transfers authorised by the BNA.

The regime decides how IVA is calculated. The general regime applies to entities with annual turnover or imports above Kz 350 million. The simplified regime covers turnover or imports between Kz 25 million and Kz 350 million, with its own calculation. When turnover passes a threshold, the 2026 budget law requires the company to change regime by the end of the month after the triggering operation. Your accountant must confirm which regime applies.

::: tip | Check the current law
IVA rates, product tables and regime thresholds change through budget laws. Check the IVA Code and the AGT site before you set up a product rate in your billing system.
:::

## 3. Invoices and the right to deduct

Input IVA is deductible only when it is supported by a valid invoice and linked to the company's taxable activity.

::: slides
SLIDE Check 1: Supplier details | Name, address and NIF of the supplier | A real, registered supplier
SLIDE Check 2: Buyer details | Your company's exact name and NIF | Wrong NIF, no deduction
SLIDE Check 3: Amounts and rate | Price, IVA rate and IVA amount shown separately | The rate must suit the product
SLIDE Check 4: Business purpose | Purchase used for taxable sales | Exempt activity gives no deduction
:::

Invoices must be issued on time. The PwC summary notes a fine of 0.2% of the value of each invoice issued late, and fines of 7% or 15% of the invoice value for invoices left out of the SAF-T file sent to the AGT. Good invoicing protects both the seller and the buyer.

::: quiz
? A supplier invoice shows IVA of Kz 70,000 but the buyer's NIF is wrong. What should the accounts clerk do?
- Deduct it, because the IVA was paid || Without a valid invoice the deduction can be rejected in an audit.
- Record the IVA as a cost and forget it || The company may recover it once the invoice is corrected.
* Hold the deduction and ask the supplier for a corrected invoice || Correct. Deduct only when the invoice is valid.
:::

## Worked example: a hardware distributor in Luanda

A hardware distributor in Cazenga, Luanda, is in the general regime and sells only standard-rated goods. In June its sales are Kz 12,000,000 before IVA, so output IVA is 14% x 12,000,000 = Kz 1,680,000. Purchases from local suppliers total Kz 7,000,000 before IVA, with Kz 980,000 of IVA. One invoice of Kz 500,000 (IVA Kz 70,000) shows another company's NIF, so the accountant excludes it until corrected. Deductible input IVA is Kz 910,000. IVA payable for June = 1,680,000 - 910,000 = Kz 770,000. The faulty invoice costs the company Kz 70,000 of cash this month until it is fixed.

::: case | A large customer asks for "no IVA"
A building contractor offers to buy Kz 3,000,000 of materials from the hardware distributor if the sales manager, Carla, issues the invoice "without IVA" and gives a 5% discount.
? What should Carla do?
- Accept, because the customer is large and pays cash || Leaving out IVA on a taxable sale is illegal, and the distributor still owes the tax.
- Issue an invoice without IVA but record IVA in the accounts || The invoice and the accounts would not match, which an audit will detect.
* Refuse to remove IVA and, if needed, negotiate the price before IVA || Correct. IVA at 14% must be charged, and commercial terms are negotiated on the net price.
- Ask the customer to pay the IVA later in cash || IVA must appear on the invoice when the sale is made.
:::

## 1. The compliance calendar

Missed deadlines usually come from relying on memory. A compliance calendar lists every obligation, its legal source, its owner and its status. The verified deadlines in this course are:

- INSS: remuneration sheet and payment by the 10th of the month following the month of pay (Decree n.º 227/18).
- IRT Group A: withheld tax delivered to the AGT by the end of the month following payment (IRT Code, article 11).
- IVA: monthly periodic return and payment. Take the exact date from the current AGT tax calendar.
- Modelo 2: the employer's annual IRT declaration, filed in February for the previous year (IRT Code, article 12).

Add company-specific items: the payroll cut-off date, the January update of tax tables, and any annual labour filings your lawyer confirms. A 2026 law-firm bulletin, for example, listed the submission of salary records to the labour inspectorate by the end of June.

::: diagram cycle | Monthly payroll compliance cycle

Approve and pay: net pay to employees
Deliver: INSS by the 10th, IRT by month end
Reconcile and file: proofs, ledgers and records

## 2. Penalties for late or missing delivery

Penalties make late compliance expensive. Verified examples from professional sources:

- IRT: under the 2026 budget law, failing to deliver withheld IRT, or delivering less, carries a fine equal to the amount not delivered, and criminal proceedings may apply.
- INSS: default interest of 1% per month on the debt, and administrative fines from one to eight times the average monthly remuneration.
- Invoices: 0.2% of the value of each invoice issued late.

Example: a company withholds Kz 1,200,000 of IRT and does not deliver it. The fine alone can equal Kz 1,200,000, so the company may pay Kz 2,400,000 for a debt of Kz 1,200,000, plus any interest.

::: tip | Check the current law
Penalty levels and deadlines were checked in 2025–2026 sources and can change through budget laws or the new IRPS code. Confirm on the AGT and INSS websites and with a qualified accountant.
:::

## 3. Four payroll controls

Payroll is a common target for error and fraud because it moves large amounts every month. Four controls prevent most problems.

::: slides
SLIDE Control 1: Segregation of duties | HR changes master data, payroll calculates, finance approves and pays | No one person controls the whole process
SLIDE Control 2: Maker-checker | A second person reviews the payroll before approval | Review the calculation, not just the total
SLIDE Control 3: Master data change control | Salary or bank changes need a signed form and approval | Most payroll fraud starts in master data
SLIDE Control 4: Variance analysis and headcount check | Compare with last month, and match HR list, payroll and INSS sheet | Every change needs a reason
:::

Variance analysis in practice: last month total net pay was Kz 18,200,000 and this month it is Kz 19,650,000, an increase of Kz 1,450,000 (8.0%). The clerk must explain the change, for example two new hires, one promotion and Saturday overtime. A headcount check matches three lists: HR active staff, payroll and the INSS remuneration sheet. A name on payroll but not in HR is a possible ghost employee.

::: quiz

- Pay all 64, then check next month || Paying first risks paying a ghost employee.
* Identify the extra name and confirm with HR whether the person is still employed || Correct. The difference must be explained before payment.
- Add the extra person to the INSS sheet || This would declare a person who may not be an employee.
:::
